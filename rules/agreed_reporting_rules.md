# Agreed reporting rules for interpretative myeloid NGS summaries

## Scope and report structure

These rules apply to a concise interpretative summary for clinical haematologists. The purpose is to explain how the detected NGS findings alter or refine the diagnosis, prognosis, management, measurable residual disease assessment or consideration of germline predisposition in the supplied clinical and morphological context.

Use the following order, omitting sections that are not relevant except for the mandatory detected-variant summary:

0. Detected NGS variants
1. Integrated diagnosis and classification
2. Prognostic significance
3. Clinically actionable implications
4. MRD implications
5. Possible germline predisposition

Do not repeat the clinical history, morphology or standard treatment unless needed to explain the effect of a molecular finding.

# R0 — Detected NGS variants

1. **What variants were detected on NGS?** This rule is mandatory and must always be reported. State only the patient-specific NGS result without interpretation. If no pathogenic variants were detected on NGS, state exactly: `No pathogenic variants were detected on NGS.`

# R1 — Diagnosis and classification

1. **How should the supplied molecular findings confirm, change, exclude or qualify the starting clinicopathological diagnosis?** Interpret variants in the supplied clinicopathological context. Do not diagnose a myeloid neoplasm from mutation number, mutation identity or VAF alone. Treat the stated morphological diagnosis as the starting point.

2. **What WHO-5 diagnosis should the final report state?** Use WHO-5 as the primary diagnostic classifier.

3. **What ICC diagnosis should the final report state, and is it materially different from the WHO-5 diagnosis?** Assess ICC separately. State the ICC diagnosis, then determine whether it is materially the same as the WHO-5 diagnosis.

4. **Does a detected alteration establish an entity-defining diagnosis, and if so what is the integrated diagnosis?** Apply the required blast range, morphology, cytogenetic findings, variant class, VAF threshold and exclusion criteria. Do not substitute a biologically related mutation for the mutation required by the entity definition.

5. **When more than one molecular or cytogenetic feature is present, which entity has diagnostic precedence and which co-mutation effects must remain separate?** Assign the entity with the appropriate classification precedence rather than listing competing diagnoses. Keep entity assignment separate from prognostic effects of co-mutations.

6. **If morphology is non-diagnostic, does the clone support CHIP, CCUS, or neither, and what neoplasm must not be overcalled?** Classify a qualifying clone as:
   - **CHIP** when cytopenia is absent or an adequate external cause explains the cytopenia; or
   - **CCUS** when cytopenia is persistent, otherwise unexplained and no myeloid neoplasm is established.

   A small clone must not be used to overcall MDS, MPN or another neoplasm.

7. **Which competing diagnoses remain relevant, and which decisive clinical or morphological variables prevent the genotype from overriding them?** A genotype may suggest a differential but does not override mandatory clinical or morphological criteria. Check decisive variables such as absolute and relative monocytosis, eosinophilia, dysgranulopoiesis, blast percentage, fibrosis, reactive causes and defining rearrangements.

8. **Which negative molecular findings are diagnostically informative enough to report, and which negative findings should be omitted?** Mention absence only when the alteration is ordinarily expected in the relevant differential, directly changes the diagnostic label, establishes triple-negative status, or helps determine allelic state. Do not list unrelated absent genes.

9. **What, if anything, does the VAF support in this case, and which clonal or germline inferences must not be made from VAF alone?** VAF may support a small, substantial or dominant clonal population, but bulk sequencing does not establish founding versus secondary status, chronological order, whether variants occur in the same cells, cis/trans phase, or germline origin.

10. **What TP53 allelic state is supported, and which TP53 allelic-state label must not be used?** Distinguish a single monoallelic mutation from multi-hit or biallelic disease. A single mutation without a qualifying second hit must not be called biallelic. Two qualifying TP53 mutations, or a mutation with a qualifying deletion/copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity, support multi-hit status under the applicable classifier.

11. **Does allele burden materially affect the diagnostic interpretation, and what must not be inferred from a low or high VAF?** Do not use a low VAF to dismiss an otherwise established diagnosis when low allele burden is biologically expected. Conversely, do not use a high VAF as a substitute for missing diagnostic criteria.

12. **Which assay-scope limitation, if any, materially affects the diagnostic interpretation and should be stated?** A negative SNV/indel panel does not exclude rearrangements, copy-number changes or variants outside validated coverage. Integrate cytogenetics, FISH, fusion testing and other assays where relevant.

13. **Does any diagnostic or therapeutic implication depend on the exact exon, codon, alteration type or fusion partner, and what precise variant-level interpretation should be reported?** Do not infer a gene-level implication when the evidence is specific to a particular molecular alteration.

# R2 — Prognostic interpretation

1. **Which disease-specific prognostic framework should be used for this case?** Prefer a validated disease-specific prognostic system where one exists. For findings not addressed by that system, use high-quality disease-specific prognostic evidence.

2. **What favourable, adverse or established-neutral prognostic contribution does each detected pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant make in this disease?** Use the applicable prognostic system first; if it does not address the variant, use high-quality disease-specific evidence.

3. **Which molecular prognostic contributions should be reported without calculating a composite clinical score or assigning its overall risk tier?** When a prognostic system incorporates non-molecular variables, report how the detected molecular findings contribute to prognosis without calculating the complete score or assigning its overall risk tier.

4. **For AML, which ELN framework or frameworks should be reported in this clinical setting, and what risk category do the available findings support?** Use ELN 2022 as the primary AML risk classification. ELN 2024 Less-Intensive may additionally be reported when it gives a materially different risk category. Reporting ELN 2024 Less-Intensive is mandatory when the patient is already receiving less-intensive treatment or is explicitly unsuitable for intensive therapy. Reporting ELN 2022 is optional if and only if ELN 2024 is mandatory.

5. **Which prognostic interpretations must be omitted because they would transfer evidence between diseases or prognostic models?** Apply a prognostic system only to the disease for which it is validated, and do not assign a variant a prognostic effect based solely on evidence from another disease.

6. **Which negative panel findings must be treated as wild-type for the selected prognostic model, and what assay-scope limit applies to that inference?** When a gene is included in the NGS panel and no reportable variant is identified, treat it as wild-type for the purpose of the selected prognostic system. Do not extend this inference beyond the validated scope of the assay.

7. **What prognostic effect follows from the established TP53 allelic state?** Distinguish monoallelic TP53 alterations from TP53 multi-hit disease and apply the prognostic effect appropriate to the established allelic state.

8. **Which prognostic evidence changes the patient-level interpretation, and which background prognostic information should be omitted?** Omit prevalence, epidemiological background and prognostic associations that do not contribute to the patient-level prognostic interpretation.

# R3 — Clinical actionability

1. **Which management implications arise specifically from the detected molecular alterations, and which standard-treatment statements should be omitted because they do not depend on the NGS result?** Do not restate standard-of-care treatment that would apply regardless of the NGS result.

2. **Which exact actionable alteration selects or supports a therapy, in which disease and treatment setting, and how established is that implication?** Specify the relevant mutation, fusion or pathway, the treatment phase where necessary, and whether the implication is established, optional or investigational.

3. **Which detected alterations modify response, resistance, relapse risk or survival for a therapy relevant to this patient?** Report treatment-specific molecular modifiers. Do not extrapolate treatment-specific effects to overall prognosis.

4. **How strongly can any molecular sensitivity or resistance claim be stated for this exact variant and evidence base?** Use qualified wording when evidence is limited, variant-specific or based on small series. “May be sensitive” is appropriate when a definitive response cannot be predicted.

5. **Which approval, treatment-line, trial or jurisdictional access context is necessary to qualify an actionable implication?** Distinguish approved frontline, relapsed/refractory, trial-only and jurisdiction-dependent uses without turning the report into a treatment protocol.

6. **Do the detected variants select an approved mutation-specific therapy, and if not, should therapy commentary be omitted?** Do not invent actionability. When the detected variants do not select an approved mutation-specific therapy, say so only if this is clinically useful; otherwise instruct the final report to omit therapy commentary.

7. **What diagnostic, prognostic and predictive role should be assigned to each relevant alteration, and which roles must not be conflated?** A mutation may define the disease or worsen prognosis without selecting a targeted drug. Conversely, a therapeutically actionable mutation may not define the diagnostic entity.

8. **Does a molecular finding materially alter transplant risk assessment, donor selection or therapeutic strategy, and if not, should a transplant recommendation be omitted?** Do not recommend transplantation solely because a mutation is present.

9. **For each relevant kinase alteration, what precise molecular class is present and which treatment implications apply to that class?** Different variants in the same gene can have different pathway activation and drug sensitivity; do not apply one mutation’s treatment logic to another.

10. **Does an actionable treatment implication arise from a cytogenetic or FISH-defined alteration outside the NGS panel, and what implication should be integrated?** Recognise defining fusions, rearrangements or deletions detected by cytogenetics or FISH when they determine treatment.

11. **If possible germline predisposition is present, which immediate disease-treatment implications must be kept separate from genetic counselling, constitutional confirmation and donor-selection implications?** Do not merge these distinct management issues.

# R4 — MRD interpretation

1. **Which detected variants, if any, are validated molecular MRD markers for this disease, assay and timepoint, and which variants should not be presented as MRD markers?** Do not assume that a diagnostic NGS variant is an MRD marker. Use only disease-, gene-, assay- and timepoint-validated MRD approaches.

2. **What can a routine-panel result of “not detected” establish about MRD, and what molecular-remission claim must not be made from it?** Distinguish routine-panel sensitivity from dedicated MRD sensitivity. “Not detected” on a routine assay means below that assay’s reportable threshold, not biological absence or molecular remission.

3. **Which validated leukaemia-specific marker should be followed, and what high-sensitivity assay should be recommended?** When a validated leukaemia-specific marker is present, identify it explicitly and recommend an appropriate high-sensitivity assay. For NPM1-mutated AML, the specific NPM1 mutation is the preferred molecular MRD target.

4. **What, if anything, should the final report say about detected DNMT3A, TET2 or ASXL1 variants in the MRD context?** Do not assign MRD status from persistent clonal-haematopoiesis-associated mutations. These variants may persist independently of active leukaemia and must not determine remission status by themselves. If they provide no positive MRD implication, instruct the final report to omit discussion of their unsuitability as MRD markers rather than reporting a negative statement.

5. **What, if anything, should the final report say about detected IDH1 or IDH2 variants in the MRD context?** Do not use IDH1 or IDH2 as stand-alone MRD markers. Persistence or clearance should not independently establish molecular remission, relapse or treatment failure. If they provide no positive MRD implication, instruct the final report to omit discussion of their unsuitability as stand-alone MRD markers.

6. **If FLT3-ITD is present, what role should it have in MRD assessment relative to any validated leukaemia-specific marker?** Use FLT3-ITD only within a validated high-sensitivity strategy. When a validated leukaemia-specific marker such as NPM1 is available, FLT3-ITD should be complementary rather than the sole follow-up marker.

7. **What, if anything, should the final report say about other non-validated mutations in the MRD context?** Do not promote spliceosome, cohesin, transcription-factor, signalling or other non-validated mutations to stand-alone MRD markers. Interpret them only within a validated multimodal strategy. If they provide no positive MRD implication, instruct the final report to omit discussion of their unsuitability as MRD markers.

8. **If no validated molecular MRD marker is available, what should the final report say or omit about molecular MRD?** Keep multiparameter flow cytometry, morphology and clinical assessment central and do not manufacture a molecular endpoint. State the absence of a validated molecular marker only when clinically useful; otherwise instruct the final report to omit molecular MRD commentary.

9. **If a residual variant is detected, what assay threshold, specimen, treatment regimen, treatment timepoint and serial kinetics are required for its interpretation?** Do not assign relapse from a single low-level result without corroboration.

10. **Does a persistent residual mutation justify treatment escalation, and what corroboration is required before any such implication is stated?** Do not escalate treatment solely because an unvalidated residual mutation remains detectable. Correlate with the validated marker, flow cytometry, morphology and clinical course.

11. **Does a disease-specific validated molecular MRD framework exist for this non-AML myeloid neoplasm, and if not, what MRD commentary should be omitted?** Do not transfer AML-specific MRD guidance to other myeloid neoplasms unless a disease-specific validated framework exists. Silence is appropriate where no validated molecular MRD recommendation applies.

# R5 — Possible germline predisposition

1. **Is possible germline origin supported by the combination of gene, variant type, VAF and personal phenotype, and what level of suspicion should be reported?** Do not rely on VAF alone.

2. **What germline-status wording is justified by tumour-only sequencing, and which definitive germline claim must not be made?** Never diagnose germline status from tumour-only sequencing. Use wording such as “possible germline,” “suspected germline” or “presumed germline pending constitutional confirmation.”

3. **Does the molecular architecture support possible germline predisposition, and what architecture supports that interpretation?** Examples include a near-heterozygous loss-of-function predisposition variant with a lower-VAF recurrent somatic second event, or a pathogenic variant associated with a longstanding constitutional phenotype.

4. **If germline predisposition is possible, what constitutional confirmation and genetic-counselling recommendation should be made, and which specimen is preferred?** Recommend confirmation using a validated non-haematopoietic specimen and genetic counselling. Cultured skin fibroblasts are preferred where blood, marrow, saliva or buccal cells may be contaminated by the haematopoietic clone.

5. **Which claims about constitutional allele identity, cis/trans phase or clonal co-occurrence must not be made from bulk VAF alone?** Do not infer which allele is constitutional, whether variants are in cis or trans, or whether two variants occur in the same clone from bulk VAF alone. Phasing or lineage-resolved testing may be required.

6. **Despite the absence of a near-50% variant, do the molecular findings, phenotype or assay limitations still justify dedicated constitutional testing?** Do not dismiss germline predisposition because no near-50% variant was detected on the myeloid panel. A recurrent low-VAF somatic “second hit,” relevant phenotype or incomplete assay coverage may still justify dedicated constitutional testing, including copy-number analysis where appropriate.

7. **If germline predisposition is confirmed, which practical implications should be stated for donor selection, family counselling and cascade testing?** Do not recommend testing relatives as though germline status were already confirmed.

8. **Does uncertainty about somatic versus germline origin alter the applicability of a prognostic model, and what provisional wording should be used?** Keep germline interpretation separate from somatic prognostic scoring. Where somatic versus germline origin changes the applicability of a prognostic model, state that the molecular risk contribution is provisional pending constitutional testing.

9. **Does this case warrant germline-predisposition commentary, and if not, what germline discussion should the final report omit?** Avoid indiscriminate germline flagging. A common somatic hotspot at a plausible somatic VAF, without a compatible phenotype or predisposition-gene context, should not trigger routine germline recommendations. If there is no supported germline concern, instruct the final report to omit germline commentary rather than explaining why germline predisposition is unlikely.

# Style requirements

- Lead with the clinically important conclusion.
- Be concise and specific.
- Explain only the molecular facts that change diagnosis, prognosis, management, MRD interpretation or germline assessment.
- Distinguish established findings from possibilities and uncertainties.
- Do not speculate beyond the supplied data.
- Do not fabricate literature, evidence, thresholds, assay performance or treatment approvals.
