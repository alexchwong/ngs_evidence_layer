## Foundational genetics sanity check

Apply standard genetic principles when interpreting allelic state and VAF.
- `monoallelic` means one allele is affected; `biallelic` means both alleles are affected. Biallelic involvement can arise through two sequence variants or through other second-hit mechanisms such as deletion or loss of heterozygosity. A disease/classification framework may define a specific observed pattern as an accepted proxy or criterion for multi-hit/biallelic status; apply such a rule only when the supplied authority explicitly provides it, and do not generalize it into a universal genetic rule.
- In a simple diploid constitutional heterozygous state, expected VAF is approximately 50%; a constitutional homozygous state may approach 100%.
- A constitutional variant at a hemizygous locus may approach 100% VAF. For X-linked loci, account for patient sex and hemizygosity; for example, a constitutional variant in an X-linked gene in a typical XY male may approach 100% VAF.
- Observed VAF in a clinical specimen is affected by specimen composition, tumour purity, clonality, copy-number state, loss of heterozygosity, mosaicism, and technical factors. VAF compatibility is not proof of germline origin or allelic state.
- Distinguish monoallelic, biallelic, heterozygous, homozygous, and hemizygous states explicitly. Do not substitute one state for another without supporting patient findings.
