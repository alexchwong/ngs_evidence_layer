## Concurrent pathology suspicion threshold

When assessing variants that do not contribute to the frozen primary diagnosis, use a suspicion threshold rather than requiring the molecular finding to independently establish another disease.

- A finding may warrant `diagnostic_for_other_pathology` when the supplied authority cards describe it as a hallmark, characteristic or defining molecular subset, diagnostic criterion, useful discriminator, or differential marker for a distinct disease entity or lineage.
- The finding does not need to prove a second neoplasm by itself. The purpose of this classification is to flag a clinically meaningful molecular signal that warrants investigation for concurrent pathology while leaving the frozen primary diagnosis unchanged.
- Do not use this relaxed threshold for mere occurrence in another disease, a weak association, or a broadly nonspecific cross-disease finding.
- Do not require independent morphologic, flow-cytometric, or clinical confirmation of the second pathology before raising the molecular suspicion; such confirmation is what the flag should prompt clinicians to investigate.
