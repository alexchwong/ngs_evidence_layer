# Wang F, Morita K, DiNardo CD, Furudate K, Tanaka T, Yan Y, et al. Leukemia stemness and co-occurring mutations drive resistance to IDH inhibitors in acute myeloid leukemia. Nature Communications. 2021 May 10;12(1):2607.

**Publication key:** `idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance`

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0001

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** IDH1, IDH2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Introduction

### Interpretation

In relapsed/refractory AML with mutant IDH1 or IDH2, allosteric IDH inhibitors (ivosidenib for IDH1 and enasidenib for IDH2) suppress 2HG and were reported to produce an overall response rate of about 40%, with response largely mediated by terminal differentiation rather than obligatory clearance of the IDH-mutant clone.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0002

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** RUNX1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Discussion—co-occurring mutations and IDHi resistance

### Interpretation

In this IDH-inhibitor-treated AML cohort, baseline RUNX1 co-mutation was associated with a lower complete-remission rate, and RUNX1 was also among the mutations most frequently acquired or selected at relapse.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0003

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** NRAS

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Co-occurring RUNX1 or RAS signaling mutations are associated with primary resistance to IDH inhibitors

### Interpretation

Among IDH-inhibitor-treated AML patients in this cohort, none of the six patients with concurrent NRAS mutations achieved complete remission; the paper notes prior association of NRAS with poor enasidenib response.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0004

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** TP53, FLT3

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Co-occurring RUNX1 or RAS signaling mutations are associated with primary resistance to IDH inhibitors

### Interpretation

In this IDH-inhibitor-treated AML cohort, none of the three patients with co-occurring TP53 mutations and none of the three with co-occurring FLT3 mutations responded, but these associations were not statistically significant because of the small numbers.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0005

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** RUNX1, CEBPA, GATA2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Co-occurring RUNX1 or RAS signaling mutations are associated with primary resistance to IDH inhibitors

### Interpretation

In this IDH-inhibitor-treated AML cohort, co-occurring mutations in the hematopoietic differentiation transcription-factor genes RUNX1, CEBPA and GATA2 were associated with a significantly worse complete-remission rate (P=0.037).

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0006

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** STAG2, SMC1A, RAD21

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Co-occurring RUNX1 or RAS signaling mutations are associated with primary resistance to IDH inhibitors

### Interpretation

In this IDH-inhibitor-treated AML cohort, co-occurring cohesin-gene mutations in STAG2, SMC1A and RAD21 showed only a trend toward better response (P=0.083), not a statistically significant benefit.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0007

**Category:** biomarker

**Genes:** DNMT3B, ZBTB46, NYNRIN, ARHGAP22, LAPTM4B, MMRN1, DPYSL3, KIAA0125, CDK6, CPXM1, SOCS2, SMIM24, EMP1, NGFRAP1, CD34, AKR1C3, GPR56

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** multivariable-adjusted

**Locator:** Results—Leukemia stemness is associated with primary resistance to IDHi; Methods—LSC17 score calculation

### Interpretation

In IDH1/IDH2-mutant AML treated with IDH inhibitors, a higher 17-gene LSC17 stemness score was associated with nonresponse and predicted complete remission (AUROC 0.785, P=0.028); in multivariable logistic regression it was the significant response-predicting covariate, supporting LSC17 as a potential predictive biomarker that requires independent validation.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0008

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** IDH1, IDH2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Clonal selection of driver mutations frequently accompanies relapse after IDHi

### Interpretation

Among responders to IDH-inhibitor therapy, clearance or marked reduction of the IDH mutation at response occurred in a minority; patients with IDH mutation clearance had better relapse-free survival than those without clearance, while baseline VAF and IDH mutation type did not predict clearance.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0009

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** BCOR, RUNX1, KRAS, NRAS

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Clonal selection of driver mutations frequently accompanies relapse after IDHi

### Interpretation

At relapse after IDH-inhibitor therapy, emerging or selected mutations were found in 16 of 18 paired cases; BCOR was acquired/selected in four cases and RUNX1, KRAS and NRAS in three each, supporting these alterations as acquired-resistance-associated changes in this AML series.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0010

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** KRAS, NRAS, IDH2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Clonal selection of driver mutations frequently accompanies relapse after IDHi

### Interpretation

In one relapsed case (UPN2394529), emerging KRAS p.Q61H and NRAS p.G12S mutations were in clones independent of the IDH2-mutant clone by single-cell DNA sequencing, showing that selection of a non-IDH clone can drive relapse during IDH-inhibitor therapy.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0011

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** TET2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Discussion—acquired resistance mechanisms

### Interpretation

Acquisition of TET2 mutation was identified as a likely IDH-inhibitor resistance mechanism; in the TET2-acquired relapse cases, plasma 2HG remained suppressed while DNA hypomethylation failed to occur.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0012

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** IDH1, IDH2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results—Mapping genetic and epigenetic evolution during IDHi therapy in individual cases

### Interpretation

One patient with an IDH2-mutant malignancy treated with AG221 relapsed with emergent IDH1 p.R132C; 2HG and methylation rose at relapse, consistent with IDH homolog switching as an acquired IDH-inhibitor resistance mechanism.

## idh-comutations-inhibitor-resistance-C0013

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** BCOR

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Discussion—acquired resistance mechanisms

### Interpretation

Loss-of-function BCOR mutations were frequently acquired at relapse after IDH-inhibitor therapy, but the paper states that how BCOR loss contributes to resistance remains unclear.
