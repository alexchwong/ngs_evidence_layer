# Feurstein S, Hahn CN, Mehta N, Godley LA. A practical guide to interpreting germline variants that drive hematopoietic malignancies, bone marrow failure, and chronic cytopenias. Genetics in Medicine. 2022;24(4):931-954.

**Publication key:** `feurstein-2021-myeloid-germline`

## feurstein-2021-myeloid-germline-C0001

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** DDX41

**Diseases:** MDS, AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Materials and Methods — Calculation of minor allele frequency thresholds, DDX41 example

### Interpretation

Heterozygous germline DDX41 variants predispose to MDS and AML with incomplete penetrance estimated at approximately 62%; for variant interpretation, the authors calculate a BA1 MAF threshold of 0.00109 (0.1%) and a BS1 range of 0.000109–0.00109 (0.01%–0.1%), and recommend applying these cutoffs to the total gnomAD population rather than subpopulation MAFs because founder variants are present.

## feurstein-2021-myeloid-germline-C0002

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** CEBPA

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Materials and Methods — Calculation of number of probands for PS4, CEBPA example

### Interpretation

Germline CEBPA variants cause hereditary AML without other organ manifestations, with a reported median age of onset of 24 years; germline CEBPA heterozygotes with AML almost always acquire a second CEBPA variant in the constitutionally wild-type allele and therefore present with biallelic CEBPA variants.

## feurstein-2021-myeloid-germline-C0003

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** BRAF, CARD11, CBL, ETV6, IKZF1, KRAS, MPL, NRAS, PTPN11, RAD51, RIT1, STAT3, TP53

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Results — Observation in multiple probands (PS4), population-database CHIP caveat

### Interpretation

When evaluating putative germline variants in CHIP-associated BRAF, CARD11, CBL, ETV6, IKZF1, KRAS, MPL, NRAS, PTPN11, RAD51, RIT1, STAT3, or TP53, gnomAD observations may represent CHIP rather than constitutional alleles and this possibility should be considered; in the cited TP53 analysis, a germline VAF filter >0.2 did not exclude somatic acquired or mosaic variants.

## feurstein-2021-myeloid-germline-C0004

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** GATA2

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results — Protein domains and constraint scores, GATA2 somatic-versus-germline hotspot example

### Interpretation

Use of somatic mutational hotspots for PM1 requires gene-specific evaluation and should be conservative, with somatic hotspots used when no germline hotspot is known; for GATA2, missense variants in zinc finger 1 are a typical somatic hotspot, whereas missense variants in zinc finger 2 are a common germline hotspot associated with clinical GATA2 deficiency.

## feurstein-2021-myeloid-germline-C0007

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** ETV6

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results — Functional criteria, ETV6 functional-assay recommendations

### Interpretation

For inherited ETV6 variant curation, the authors support combining distinct functional readouts including decreased transcriptional repression, impaired megakaryocytic differentiation, and aberrant cytoplasmic localization; they propose that two different supporting functional assays can reach moderate strength, while one moderate plus one supporting assay or three supporting assays can reach strong evidence.

## feurstein-2021-myeloid-germline-C0008

**Genes:** ANKRD26, GATA2, TERC

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results — Figure 5, germline-testing workflow footnote on high-yield noncoding coverage

### Interpretation

For germline testing in hematopoietic malignancies, bone marrow failure, and chronic cytopenias, spike-in probes for noncoding regions of interest should cover high-yield regions including the ANKRD26 5′UTR, the GATA2 intronic enhancer, and the TERC RNA gene.
