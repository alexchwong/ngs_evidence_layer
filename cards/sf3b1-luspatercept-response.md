# Platzbecker U, Germing U, Götze KS, Kiewe P, Mayer K, Chromik J, et al. Luspatercept for the treatment of anaemia in patients with lower-risk myelodysplastic syndromes (PACE-MDS): a multicentre, open-label phase 2 dose-finding study with long-term extension study. The Lancet Oncology. 2017;18(10):1338-1347.

**Publication key:** `sf3b1-luspatercept-response`

## sf3b1-luspatercept-response-C0001

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** SF3B1

**Diseases:** MDS

**Evidence tier:** multivariable-adjusted

**Locator:** Results, SF3B1 molecular response analysis and multivariable logistic regression; Discussion, efficacy summary

### Interpretation

Among patients with lower-risk MDS treated with higher-dose luspatercept, SF3B1 mutation was associated with a higher erythroid response rate (IWG HI-E: 77% vs 40% in mutation-negative patients), and SF3B1 mutation status remained significant in multivariable analysis (p=0.01).

## sf3b1-luspatercept-response-C0002

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1, ZRSR2

**Diseases:** MDS

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Table 2, any splicing factor subgroup, IWG HI-E column

### Interpretation

Among patients with lower-risk MDS treated with luspatercept 0.75–1.75 mg/kg, International Working Group haematological improvement–erythroid (IWG HI-E) occurred in 73% with a mutation in any assessed splicing factor (SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1, or ZRSR2) versus 36% without an assessed splicing-factor mutation.

## sf3b1-luspatercept-response-C0003

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1, ZRSR2

**Diseases:** MDS

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Table 2, any splicing factor subgroup, RBC-TI column and footnote

### Interpretation

Among RBC-transfusion-independence-evaluable patients with lower-risk MDS treated with luspatercept 0.75–1.75 mg/kg, red blood cell transfusion independence (RBC-TI) for at least 8 weeks occurred in 50% with a mutation in any assessed splicing factor (SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1, or ZRSR2) versus 8% without an assessed splicing-factor mutation.
