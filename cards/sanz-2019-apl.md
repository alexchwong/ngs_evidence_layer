## sanz-2019-apl-C0003

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** PML, RARA, ZBTB16, NPM1, NUMA1, STAT5B, PRKAR1A, FIP1L1, BCOR, OBFC2A, TBLR1, GTF2I, IRF2BP2, FNDC3B

**Diseases:** APL

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Table 1, recommendation 1.3; Management of special situations and APL molecular variants; Table 4

### Interpretation

According to the ELN recommendation, APL diagnosis should be confirmed by molecular detection of PML::RARA or a recognized rare RARA fusion; reported rare fusions include ZBTB16::RARA, NPM1::RARA, NUMA1::RARA, STAT5B::RARA, PRKAR1A::RARA, FIP1L1::RARA, BCOR::RARA, OBFC2A::RARA, TBLR1::RARA, GTF2I::RARA, IRF2BP2::RARA, and FNDC3B::RARA.

## sanz-2019-apl-C0095

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** 

**Diseases:** APL

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Treatment options > Non-high-risk patients; Table 2, recommendation 2.2

### Interpretation

For newly diagnosed non–high-risk APL with presenting WBC ≤10 × 10^9/L, ATRA plus ATO without chemotherapy is the recommended first-line treatment; ATRA plus anthracycline-based chemotherapy is an alternative when ATO is contraindicated or unaffordable.

## sanz-2019-apl-C0097

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** 

**Diseases:** APL

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Treatment options > High-risk patients; Table 2, recommendation 2.3

### Interpretation

For newly diagnosed high-risk APL with presenting WBC >10 × 10^9/L, two valid first-line approaches are ATRA plus ATO with cytoreductive chemotherapy or conventional ATRA plus anthracycline-based chemotherapy.
