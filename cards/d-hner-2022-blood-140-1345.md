# Döhner H, Wei AH, Appelbaum FR, Craddock C, DiNardo CD, Dombret H, et al. Diagnosis and management of AML in adults: 2022 recommendations from an international expert panel on behalf of the ELN. Blood. 2022;140(12):1345-1377.

**Publication key:** `d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345`

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0001

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** BCR, ABL1, PML, RARA, CBFB, MYH11, RUNX1, RUNX1T1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Changes to the blast thresholds defining AML, paragraph 1 (paper.md lines 39-39)

### Interpretation

Under the ICC framework used in this paper, recurrent AML-defining genetic abnormalities establish AML at 10% or more marrow or blood blasts, except BCR::ABL1 AML, which requires 20% blasts; PML::RARA, CBFB::MYH11, and RUNX1::RUNX1T1 are included among the 10% threshold abnormalities.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0002

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** RARA, KMT2A, MECOM, CEBPA

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** AML with recurrent genetic abnormalities (paper.md line 51)

### Interpretation

Variant translocations involving RARA, KMT2A, or MECOM are recognized as AML-defining entities; an in-frame CEBPA bZIP mutation, whether monoallelic or biallelic, defines the CEBPA-mutated AML subtype and is associated with favorable outcome.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0003

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** TP53

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** AML with mutated TP53 (paper.md line 55)

### Interpretation

A pathogenic TP53 mutation with variant allele fraction at least 10%, with or without loss of the wild-type allele, defines AML with mutated TP53; the paper describes these neoplasms as having a very poor prognosis.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0004

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** ASXL1, BCOR, EZH2, RUNX1, SF3B1, SRSF2, STAG2, U2AF1, ZRSR2, TP53

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** AML with myelodysplasia-related gene mutations (paper.md line 57)

### Interpretation

In cases lacking TP53 mutation, pathogenic mutations in ASXL1, BCOR, EZH2, RUNX1, SF3B1, SRSF2, STAG2, U2AF1, or ZRSR2 classify AML as AML with myelodysplasia-related gene mutations irrespective of prior MDS; these mutations confer adverse prognosis even in de novo AML.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0005

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** TP53, ASXL1, BCOR, EZH2, RUNX1, SF3B1, SRSF2, STAG2, U2AF1, ZRSR2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Figure 1 caption: Hierarchical classification of the ICC of AML (paper.md line 81)

### Interpretation

The ICC classification is hierarchical. In the absence of an AML-defining recurrent genetic abnormality, AML with mutated TP53 takes precedence over AML with myelodysplasia-related gene mutations.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0011

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Molecular MRD (paper.md lines 708-714)

Validated qPCR molecular MRD targets in AML include mutated NPM1 and the CBFB::MYH11 and RUNX1::RUNX1T1 fusions; KMT2A::MLLT3, DEK::NUP214, BCR::ABL1, and WT1 are described as less validated targets.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0012

**Category:** biomarker

**Genes:** DNMT3A, TET2, ASXL1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Molecular MRD: NGS exclusions (paper.md lines 712-716)

### Interpretation

DNMT3A, TET2, and ASXL1 mutations associated with premalignant clonal hematopoiesis should not be interpreted as AML MRD; NGS-based MRD was not standardized as a stand-alone method.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0013

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** BCL2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Patients not considered candidates for intensive therapy (paper.md line 881)

### Interpretation

For older or otherwise unfit patients with AML, adding the BCL2 inhibitor venetoclax to azacitidine improved CR/CRi and median overall survival versus azacitidine alone and established a standard nonintensive treatment option.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0014

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** IDH1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** IDH-mutated AML treatment (paper.md line 891)

### Interpretation

For newly diagnosed IDH1-mutated AML, ivosidenib plus azacitidine improved event-free survival, response, and overall survival versus azacitidine plus placebo; rapid IDH1 screening is recommended in older patients to identify candidates.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0017

**Genes:** DDX41

**Diseases:** germline predisposition syndrome

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Germline predisposition (paper.md line 139)

### Interpretation

Germline predisposition should be considered in hematologic malignancy at any age, including older patients with possible DDX41 predisposition; confirmation should use nonhematopoietic material such as cultured skin fibroblasts, and only pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants are considered causative.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0018

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** RUNX1, CEBPA

**Diseases:** germline predisposition syndrome

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Germline predisposition and donor selection (paper.md lines 143-145)

### Interpretation

Germline risk testing should occur early when allogeneic transplantation is contemplated; related donors carrying deleterious RUNX1 or CEBPA variants should not be used as hematopoietic stem-cell donors.

## d-hner-2022-blood-140-1345-C0019

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, TP53, CHEK2

**Diseases:** AML, germline predisposition syndrome

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Therapy-related AML, familial cancer-predisposition paragraph (paper.md lines 85 and 131)

**Secondary citation:** Churpek JE, Marquez R, Neistadt B, et al. Inherited mutations in cancer susceptibility genes are common among breast cancer survivors who develop therapy-related leukemia. Cancer. 2016;122(2):304-311.

### Interpretation

In the therapy-related AML context, the ELN report states that deleterious familial cancer-predisposition mutations involving BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, TP53, or CHEK2 have been observed in approximately 20% of reported cases. Identifying such a preexisting condition informs counselling, family-donor selection for allogeneic HCT, cancer or organ surveillance, and cascade testing.
