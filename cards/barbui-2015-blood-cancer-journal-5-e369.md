# Barbui T, Vannucchi AM, Buxhofer-Ausch V, De Stefano V, Betti S, Rambaldi A, et al. Practice-relevant revision of IPSET-thrombosis based on 1019 patients with WHO-defined essential thrombocythemia. Blood Cancer Journal. 2015;5(11):e369-e369.

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** CALR

**Diseases:** ET

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Opening paragraph: prior IPSET-thrombosis model and CALR mutation

### Interpretation

In essential thrombocythaemia, the cited prior analysis reported that CALR mutation did not further affect the IPSET-thrombosis model for predicting future vascular events.

## barbui-2015-blood-cancer-journal-5-e369-C0002

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** JAK2

**Diseases:** ET

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results subsection (i): conventionally assigned low-risk group

### Interpretation

Among conventionally assigned low-risk ET patients, annual thrombosis rates were 0.44% with neither JAK2 mutation nor cardiovascular risk factors, 1.05% with cardiovascular risk factors alone, 1.59% with JAK2 mutation alone, and 2.57% with both; adding cardiovascular risk factors to JAK2 mutation did not significantly increase risk (P=0.217).

## barbui-2015-blood-cancer-journal-5-e369-C0003

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** JAK2

**Diseases:** ET

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Discussion: proposed revised four-category risk stratification

### Interpretation

For ET, the authors proposed that a revised thrombosis-risk scheme might include four categories: very low risk, defined by no thrombosis history, age ≤60 years and JAK2-unmutated status; low risk, defined by no thrombosis history, age ≤60 years and JAK2-mutated status; intermediate risk, defined by no thrombosis history, age >60 years and JAK2-unmutated status; and high risk, defined by thrombosis history or age >60 years with JAK2 mutation.

## barbui-2015-blood-cancer-journal-5-e369-C0004

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** JAK2

**Diseases:** ET

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Additional analysis and discussion: high-risk status defined by thrombosis history versus age alone

### Interpretation

In conventionally high-risk ET, JAK2 mutation, cardiovascular risk factors, or both provided limited additional thrombosis-risk enhancement when high-risk status was defined by thrombosis history, regardless of age (P=NS). In contrast, JAK2 mutation, with or without cardiovascular risk factors, might have affected thrombosis risk when high-risk status was defined by age alone (P=0.05).

## barbui-2015-blood-cancer-journal-5-e369-C0005

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** JAK2

**Diseases:** ET

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Results subsection (ii): conventionally assigned high-risk group

### Interpretation

Among conventionally assigned high-risk ET patients, annual thrombosis rates were 1.44% with neither JAK2 mutation nor cardiovascular risk factors, 1.64% with cardiovascular risk factors alone, 2.36% with JAK2 mutation alone, and 4.17% with both; the combined-risk group had significantly greater risk than patients with neither factor (P=0.011).

## barbui-2015-blood-cancer-journal-5-e369-C0006

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** JAK2

**Diseases:** ET

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Discussion and treatment recommendations: very-low-risk ET

### Interpretation

Pending prospective controlled studies, the authors would not insist on aspirin for very-low-risk ET (no thrombosis history, age ≤60 years and JAK2-unmutated) without cardiovascular risk factors, but advised once-daily aspirin when cardiovascular risk factors are present.

## barbui-2015-blood-cancer-journal-5-e369-C0007

**Category:** treatment

**Genes:** JAK2

**Diseases:** ET

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Treatment recommendations paragraph: low-risk JAK2-mutated ET

### Interpretation

Pending prospective controlled studies, twice-daily aspirin may reasonably be considered, but is not mandated, for low-risk JAK2-mutated ET, particularly when cardiovascular risk factors are present.
