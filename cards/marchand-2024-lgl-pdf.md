## marchand-2024-lgl-pdf-C0019

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** STAT3, STAT5B

**Diseases:** T-LGLL

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Diagnosis > Figure 4; Pathophysiology > A key role of JAK-STAT signaling

### Interpretation

In suspected T-LGLL, recurrent STAT3 or STAT5B mutations can provide molecular support for the diagnosis in the appropriate clinicophenotypic and clonal context. STAT3 mutations are typically gain-of-function SH2-domain missense variants, most commonly D661Y and Y640F; STAT5B mutations are particularly enriched in CD4-positive T-LGLL.

## marchand-2024-lgl-pdf-C0020

**Category:** diagnosis

**Genes:** STAT3, STAT5B, TET2, CCL22

**Diseases:** NK-LGLL

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Diagnosis > Figure 4; Diagnosis > Clonality assessment; Pathophysiology > genetic landscape

### Interpretation

In suspected NK-LGLL, recurrent STAT3, STAT5B, TET2 or CCL22 mutations can provide molecular support for clonality and diagnosis in the appropriate clinicophenotypic context. STAT3 mutations are typically gain-of-function SH2-domain variants including D661Y and Y640F, while CCL22 mutations are described as recurrent gain-of-function alterations. TET2 mutations are less specific because they may arise from clonal hematopoiesis or an associated myeloid clone.
