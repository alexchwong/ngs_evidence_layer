**Category:** germline

**Genes:** DDX41

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Identifying potential germline alterations

### Interpretation

Advanced age does not exclude DDX41-associated germline predisposition; the review notes that the average age at diagnosis of a myeloid malignancy in patients with germline DDX41 mutations is approximately 70 years.

## kraft-godley-2020-germline-guide-C0003

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** DDX41

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Identifying potential germline alleles from NGS assays of tumor samples

### Interpretation

A truncating DDX41 variant detected on tumor or hematopoietic NGS should raise suspicion for germline origin: the review states that all truncating DDX41 variants reported to date were germline, while suspected germline status still requires confirmation using true germline DNA.

## kraft-godley-2020-germline-guide-C0004

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** RUNX1, TP53

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Identifying potential germline alleles from NGS assays of tumor samples

### Interpretation

Many RUNX1 and TP53 variants cannot be assigned as somatic or germline from tumor-based NGS alone because the same variant may occur in either state; suspected germline findings should be confirmed using true germline DNA.

## kraft-godley-2020-germline-guide-C0011

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** TP53

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Figure 4 and accompanying text

### Interpretation

TP53 mutations are most commonly somatic, but the review notes that rare germline TP53 mutations can be recognized by tracking VAF over time or across multiple assays, including in individuals who may not meet clinical criteria for Li-Fraumeni syndrome.

## kraft-godley-2020-germline-guide-C0012

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** BRCA1, BRCA2

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Figure 4 and accompanying text

### Interpretation

In the authors’ experience, deleterious BRCA1/2 variants are more commonly germline alterations, and serial VAF assessment is an efficient screening approach; however, a stable VAF near 50% can also reflect persistent disease, clonal hematopoiesis, copy-number variation, or loss of heterozygosity, and germline origin does not itself establish pathogenicity.

## kraft-godley-2020-germline-guide-C0013

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** RUNX1, CEBPA

**Diseases:** —

**Evidence tier:** restated secondary

**Locator:** Testing related HSCT donors

### Interpretation

For deleterious germline RUNX1 or CEBPA variants, the review advises against using related donors who carry the variant because reported outcomes include poor stem-cell mobilization, engraftment or immune problems, early relapse, donor-derived leukemia, and leukemia in the donor after mobilization/collection; evidence for other genes is limited.
