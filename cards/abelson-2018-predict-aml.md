## abelson-2018-predict-aml-C0001

**Category:** biomarker

**Genes:** DNMT3A, TET2, TP53, U2AF1, SRSF2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** multivariable-adjusted

**Locator:** Abstract; main-text Fig. 3 risk-model discussion; Methods, Predictive modelling; paragraph beginning “Although our predictive model performs well”

### Interpretation

In pre-AML cases and age-/gender-matched controls sampled before diagnosis, deep-sequencing genetic parameters were used in a validated model to predict AML-free survival. The Cox model used age, gender and VAF of putative driver mutations, and the paper specifically demonstrates differing gene-level risk contributions for DNMT3A, TET2, TP53, U2AF1 and SRSF2. Because AML incidence is low, the authors caution that unselected population screening with this approach would yield many false positives.

## abelson-2018-predict-aml-C0002

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** TP53, U2AF1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** multivariable-adjusted

**Locator:** Main text and Fig. 3 legend, future-AML risk model; Methods, Predictive modelling

### Interpretation

In the combined discovery/validation pre-AML-control cohorts, TP53 and U2AF1 mutations had large gene-level effects on future AML risk: hazard ratios were 12.5 (95% CI 5.0–160.5) and 7.9 (95% CI 4.1–192.2), respectively. In Fig. 3 these gene hazard ratios represent 10-year AML risk per 5% increase in mutation VAF; the Cox analysis used age, gender and driver-mutation VAFs.

## abelson-2018-predict-aml-C0003

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** DNMT3A, TET2

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** multivariable-adjusted

**Locator:** Main text, Fig. 3 risk-model discussion; Methods, Predictive modelling

### Interpretation

In the pre-AML-control genetic risk analysis, DNMT3A and TET2 were lower-risk exceptions: whereas driver mutations in most genes conferred an approximately twofold increase in future AML risk per 5% increase in clone size, DNMT3A and TET2 conferred lower risk of progression to AML. The Cox analysis used age, gender and driver-mutation VAFs.

## abelson-2018-predict-aml-C0006

**Category:** germline

**Genes:** RUNX1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** univariable or descriptive

**Locator:** Fig. 3 legend; Methods, Curation of oncogenic variants

### Interpretation

The RUNX1 hazard ratio in this unmatched-sequencing future-AML risk analysis requires caution because deleterious germline RUNX1 variants may not be readily distinguishable from somatic mutations. More broadly, the driver-curation strategy acknowledges a small risk of including germline variants in familial AML genes when matched constitutional DNA is unavailable.
