**Genes:** NPM1

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** Table 1 recommendation A6; A3 MRD relapse

### Interpretation

For mutated-NPM1 AML, suspected MRD relapse should be confirmed by repeat paired peripheral-blood and bone-marrow testing as soon as possible, preferably within 4 weeks; a bone-marrow NPM1/ABL1 ratio ≥1% may indicate such imminent relapse that confirmation cannot safely be awaited.

## cloos-2026-blood-147-1147-C0016

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** CEBPA

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** B2 MFC-MRD technology; recommendation B13

### Interpretation

In CEBPA-mutated AML, the relevant subgroup is now defined by an in-frame bZIP CEBPA mutation; biallelic CEBPA mutations are no longer required. ## cloos-2026-blood-147-1147-C0029

**Genes:** NPM1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** C1 mutNPM1 MRD

### Interpretation

For mutated-NPM1 AML, MRD levels are approximately tenfold higher in bone marrow than peripheral blood; MRD negativity is defined as <0.001% NPM1/ABL1 or by the EAC criterion with at least 10,000 ABL1 copies, and MRD-LL thresholds therefore differ by tissue.

## cloos-2026-blood-147-1147-C0030

**Genes:** NPM1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** C1 mutNPM1 MRD

### Interpretation

After two chemotherapy cycles in mutated-NPM1 AML, formal qPCR MRD positivity is defined in peripheral blood rather than bone marrow because bone-marrow positivity does not sufficiently discriminate risk; a positive peripheral-blood result is classified as warning rather than high risk of treatment failure.

## cloos-2026-blood-147-1147-C0031

**Category:** prognosis

**Genes:** NPM1

**Diseases:** AML

**Evidence tier:** guideline criterion

**Locator:** C1 mutNPM1 MRD

### Interpretation

At end of treatment in mutated-NPM1 AML, the recommended bone-marrow NPM1/ABL1 positivity threshold is 0.1%; this threshold has prognostic significance and replaces the former 2% threshold, which often identifies patients already near hematologic relapse.
